What We Handle

Siding replacement is really a full exterior system.

In the Pacific Northwest, siding has to do more than look good. It needs the right clearances, weather barrier, flashing details, rainscreen planning, trim transitions, and product choice for rain, shade, and long damp seasons.

Breeze Siding helps homeowners compare repair versus replacement, decide where James Hardie or fiber cement makes sense, and coordinate windows, paint, trim, or deck details when the project touches more than one part of the exterior.

Common project scopes

  • Full siding tear-off and replacement
  • James Hardie and fiber cement installation
  • Hardie panel siding and modern accents
  • Window trim and flashing coordination
  • Rainscreen and drainage-plane planning for wet or shaded walls
  • Dry rot repair discovered during siding work

Warning Signs

Most siding problems start in the small exterior details.

Homeowners often call after seeing swollen boards, peeling paint, soft trim, gaps around windows, water stains, or siding that keeps needing patch repairs. Those symptoms matter because the visible siding may only be part of the issue.

A useful estimate should help you understand what is cosmetic, what may need repair behind the siding, and which details should be corrected before the new exterior goes on.

What we look for

  • Soft trim around windows, doors, corners, and fascia
  • Water paths near decks, roof returns, vents, and penetrations
  • Low siding clearances near soil, stairs, patios, and walkways
  • Past repairs that do not match the rest of the exterior
  • Shaded walls that stay damp through the wet season

Materials and Choices

The best siding choice depends on the home, not just the product name.

James Hardie and fiber cement are strong choices for many Puget Sound homes because they can handle wet weather well when installed with the right clearances, trim, flashing, and paint details. Some projects also benefit from cedar accents, panel siding, or a more traditional lap siding layout.

We help you think through durability, maintenance, curb appeal, budget, and what needs to happen behind the finished siding.

Helpful planning questions

  • Is the goal repair, curb appeal, lower maintenance, or all three?
  • Should windows, paint, or trim be included now?
  • Are there areas where water has already caused damage?
  • Do you want a modern panel look, traditional lap siding, cedar details, or a mixed exterior?
