Seal failure and replacement planning

Cost depends on what actually failed.

This page keeps the useful parts of the window seal cost topic and separates glass seal failure from active water intrusion around the exterior opening.

Cost factors

  • Glass-only vs full unit
  • Window size and style
  • Frame condition
  • Exterior trim access
  • Siding or flashing repairs

What a failed window seal looks like

Fog, haze, or moisture between panes usually means the insulated glass seal has failed. That issue affects appearance and efficiency, but it is not always the same as rainwater leaking into the wall.

When exterior repair is involved

If the frame, trim, siding, or sheathing around the window is soft or stained, the project may involve more than glass replacement. The opening may need flashing or siding repairs before it is closed up.

When full replacement makes sense

Full window replacement may be the better value when the unit is old, the frame is damaged, the operation is poor, or siding work is already planned around the opening.

Before pricing the repair, it helps to separate glass failure, frame failure, and exterior water management.
