Behind the siding

A good exterior does not rely on siding alone to keep water out.

House wrap, flashing, tape, penetrations, window openings, and clearances all work together. If one part is skipped or poorly detailed, water can find its way behind the finish even when the siding itself looks new.

Details to ask about

  • Weather barrier type
  • Window and door flashing
  • Overlap direction and seams
  • Deck and roof transitions
  • Vent and utility penetrations

What house wrap does

House wrap is designed to help shed bulk water that gets behind siding while allowing appropriate drying based on the wall system. It is not a substitute for flashing, and it should not be treated as a cure-all. It is one part of a layered exterior assembly.

Why installation quality matters

Seams should overlap correctly, penetrations should be handled carefully, and window and door openings should be integrated with flashing. The goal is to direct water down and out instead of trapping it inside the wall.

When to update it

Siding replacement is the practical time to inspect and upgrade the weather barrier. Once new siding is installed, correcting hidden wrap or flashing problems becomes much more difficult.

If an estimate only talks about the siding product and never mentions flashing or weather barrier details, ask more questions.

How this affects long-term value

In Seattle, Tacoma, and nearby communities, the weather barrier can be just as important as the finish material. A sharp-looking exterior should also be built to handle wet seasons, wind, and drying cycles.
