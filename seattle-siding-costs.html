What homeowners should know

A good siding estimate should explain the scope, not just give a number.

Two homes with similar square footage can price very differently. A simple wall with easy access is not the same as a tall exterior with failing trim, window flashing work, removal of damaged siding, and hidden rot repairs. The most useful estimate breaks those items apart so you understand what is included.

Cost factors to review

  • Siding material and profile
  • Tear-off and disposal
  • House wrap, flashing, and trim
  • Dry rot or sheathing repairs
  • Access, height, and project complexity

What affects siding replacement cost?

The biggest drivers are the size of the exterior, the siding product, the condition of the existing wall assembly, and how many details have to be rebuilt. In Seattle and Tacoma, water management matters. If the old siding failed because of poor flashing, soft trim, or trapped moisture, the replacement should address those causes instead of only covering the surface.

Material choice

Fiber cement, cedar, engineered wood, metal, and vinyl each come with different material costs, installation requirements, maintenance expectations, and appearance. The right choice depends on the home, budget, and how much long-term maintenance you want to take on.

Prep and repairs

Removing old siding can reveal damaged sheathing, missing weather barrier, failed trim, or dry rot around windows and corners. Those items should be discussed before work starts, with a clear process for documenting and pricing any hidden damage that appears during tear-off.

Trim, windows, and details

Exterior details often decide whether a project looks polished. Window trim, corner boards, belly bands, flashing, caulking strategy, and paint transitions all affect both cost and finished appearance.

A lower bid is not automatically better if it leaves out tear-off, weatherproofing, trim details, or repair allowances.

How to prepare for an estimate

  • Walk the exterior and note soft spots, swelling, cracking, or areas where paint fails early.
  • Take photos of trouble areas around windows, decks, doors, and roof-to-wall transitions.
  • Decide whether you want siding only or a coordinated siding, trim, paint, and window scope.
  • Ask what is included for cleanup, disposal, flashing, and weather barrier.
